How it works

Two ways to be taxed on the same income. The 8% flat option charges 8% on your gross sales above the ₱250,000 exemption and replaces both your income tax and the 3% percentage tax — but you must elect it, stay non-VAT registered, and keep gross at or below ₱3,000,000. The graduated option taxes your net income (gross minus expenses) on the TRAIN brackets and adds the 3% percentage tax on gross. For expenses you may use your real itemized figures or the 40% OSD — a flat optional standard deduction equal to 40% of gross, no receipts required.

The 8% option tends to win when your expenses are low relative to sales; graduated tends to win once expenses are high enough that your taxed net shrinks. This tool computes both totals so you can see the break-even for your own numbers. Above ₱3,000,000 the 8% option disappears and VAT (12%) replaces the 3% percentage tax.

  • ₱250,000 — annual exemption; the first ₱250k is untaxed under both options.
  • 8% flat rate — applied to gross receipts above ₱250,000; replaces income tax and the 3% percentage tax.
  • 3% percentage tax — Section 116, on gross, for non-VAT taxpayers with gross ≤ ₱3,000,000.
  • ₱3,000,000 — VAT threshold; above it, VAT at 12% applies instead of the 3% percentage tax and the 8% option is unavailable.
  • 40% OSD — optional standard deduction equal to 40% of gross, used in place of itemized expenses.
